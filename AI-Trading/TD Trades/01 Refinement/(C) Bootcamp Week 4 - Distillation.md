## 2. Specific Rules / Numbers / Definitions (verbatim where it matters)

### Sizing & buffer
- **Asia sizing is always HALF.** *"My sizing is always half. So I usually do 15 minis. It'll be seven, no matter what. Now, rarely I'll do 15. If I do, I'm very confident, but seven."*
- **Why half:** *"The reason why I do seven is I give it room, a lot of room actually, because I'm looking to build a buffer. So even one R, two R is perfectly okay with me."*
- **Buffer definition (functional):** banked profit earned early (usually Asia) that lets you trade New York more confidently. *"If I have a $20,000 buffer, I essentially can use that big buffer to aim for maybe $100,000 a day on one account."*
- **Buffer is NOT a license to over-risk:** *"You can still risk your one R… with a wider stop."*
- **Risk math (150k account example):** regular risk = $1,000. Building a buffer for Asia → risk **$500 instead** (half), which also gives a wider stop.
- **Don't compound the buffer into your loss limit:** *"People will use this extra buffer plus their daily loss limit… No, you can't do that."* — **You must stay JUST UNDER the buffer, never risk more than it.** (Risking buffer + daily loss limit = "full porting.")
- **Risk-free day logic:** if Asia banked 5–6R (e.g., up $2,500), in NY *"you can risk 500 today instead… there's no need to risk the full 25."* Catch an extra 3R → +$1,500 → "your biggest day, 4K."
- **Korabi's personal sizing:** *"If I have a $25,000 buffer, I'll maybe risk 10 or 15 of it. So I could do more sizing with a wider stop."*
- **Constraint:** some days he can't do 15 minis because the stop is too wide and the daily loss limit (e.g., on a Top Step account) would hit. **→ Sizing is gated by daily loss limit, not just conviction.**
- **"It's a buffer until it turns, not a buffer."** — Trail to protect; if you give it all back it was never a buffer.

### Stops & trailing
- **Asia = wider stop** by default. *"Unless it's a perfect A+ model, I'll have a super tight stop, but usually it's a wider stop."*
- Example stop in the worked Asia case: **51 points** (he mentions "86 points in between" then settles on 51 for the example).
- **He trails, does not trim:** *"As you guys know, I don't trim, I trail."*
- **Trailing rule:** look for the **new first pullback low** (in a long) and move the stop there — but only **after a buy sequence forms above the structured high.**
- **Break-even reality:** *"There's been times I'm up 20, 30K, but I have nowhere to put my stop. I'll put a break even. I'll get stopped out break even, and we keep ripping. It happens… It's Asia."*

### Structure definitions (important, verbatim)
- *"A sell sequence creates liquidity. A pullback creates the structured high."*
- *"A pullback, you just go fill an imbalance and you continue higher. It's a structured high"* (NOT liquidity).
- **Liquidity = session high/low, CPI high/low, data high/low** with strong sell sequences off them, leaving liquidity sitting → used as the **objective.**
- **"You trade INTO your objective, not OFF of your objective."** (Critical — matches the vault's "trade toward draw" rule.)
- **FVGs are guides, not entries:** *"The FVG gaps are not something for you to base your whole trade off of. It's for you to guide into the high or the low… there's multiple FVGs you can take. One of them will fail. They're supposed to guide you."*

### Sessions & time
- **NY killzone: 9:30–11:30 a.m. EST** — "fast decisive moves… best for continuation models."
- **London: 2 a.m.–5 a.m. EST** — "expect manipulation, sweeps and early reversals to indicate for New York."
- **Asia: takes its time** — rebalances or distributes, "more so in the middle."
- **Continuation window = 9:30–11:30 ET.** *"If you miss it, you miss it, go to bed, stop crying. Everything after that will be low probability."*
- **Mid-day dead zone: 11:30 a.m.–1:30 p.m.** — "no real direction usually… probability is lower."
- **Best time to trade Asia: from open for ~2–3 hours** (max 3 hours).
- **Best timeframe to read distribution vs. rebalancing: 30 minutes and above** ("a large imbalance you might need to fill, and above").
- **Liquidity levels he marks pre-session:** Asia high, Asia low, Previous Day high, Previous Day low. *"They help you build bias. Please write all this down, you will need it."*

### MFD (Macro Fueled Data) model
- **Korabi claims he created it:** *"The MFD model, I did create, yes… Continuations have been a thing for decades. Reversals have been a thing of decades. But the Macro Fueled Data model was, yeah, it was me."*
- **Track record this year:** *"the only model I've traded this year that I've not lost on once. I think I've taken three of them, four of them, and every single one has hit."*
- **What it is:** *"a clear approach to structure your trade into a news high or a news low. So CPI data high, CPI data low. One of them will get manipulated for prior. The MFD model helps you align it for you to snag that opening."*
- **Mechanics:** on the news open, *"you open up and you take the high right away. And you'll form maybe minute, two minutes, three minutes prior."* Trade the **opening candle**, straight shot for the high, then trail the rest.
- **Risk:** *"My risk is maybe 0.5R every time on a full, full-size position."* **Stop = previous wick low; target = the high.**
- **Requirement:** *"only works with high-impact news… You need to have the [draw]. If you take the [draw], you can't take it. It lowers your probability."* ⚠️ The "drop-up/draw" line is garbled — see flag in §6.

### The repeating delivery sequence (his mental model)

- **"It's the same structured approach. Every single day, nothing changes. The only thing that changes is delivery."**
