**Sequence:**
1. News releases → creates a **CPI high and CPI low** (the news wake high/low).
2. If one side fails to get taken at the start, that's fine.
3. Usually price **manipulates one side**, then **retraces and rebalances** (fills a multi-timeframe FVG).

5. Take the **first inversion with a very tight stop**, targeting the **CPI high** (opposite pool).

## The 5-step structuring framework (0:04:57)

The mentor's repeatable pre-trade sequence for news:

1. **Narrative** — your outlook (bullish/bearish), are you in an imbalance, what phase of price.
2. **Expectation** — do you expect news to displace higher or lower? ("Let's see what we do.")
3. **Release** — the news prints.
4. **Reaction** — price's immediate move.
5. **Confirmation** — the model forms (MFD model **or** continuation model).
6. **Execution** — the entry: inversion, retracement, imbalance fill, or close above a high. *"Whatever you're looking for, I don't mind nor do I care."*

## The "previous structure" continuation fallback (0:14:44–0:20:00)

**Context:** When news takes the high prior and there's nothing left to trade after ("the case for the past month").

**Rule:** *"The only thing you can do is trade the closest rebalance to structure."*

- The retracements you want are the ones **closest to a previous structure(d) high/low.**
- Modernized support/resistance: *"It's kind of the same theory, but we're implementing imbalances and order flows, maybe a little manipulation."*
- Sequence: price has a buy sequence → pullback (confirmed off a sell sequence) → that sell sequence gets recovered → expect fly above the high or manipulate it.
- The retrace zone into the previous structured high is the **point of interest** to form the model/continuation.
- **Why retail loses here:** they long too early in the imbalances ("here and here and here"), get stopped repeatedly, then price rebalances and flies. *"Just by waiting a little longer and aligning your area, it'll save you a lot of early entries."*
