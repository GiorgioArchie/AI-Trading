## Specific Rules, Numbers & Definitions (verbatim where it matters)

### The core thesis
- **"Every single morning, I wake up and I look for one thing and one thing only: objectives and liquidity."**
- **"Your objective is your liquidity."** / "Liquidity is the fire... the movement... the magnet."
- **"Price does not move randomly. It consistently and constantly seeks liquidity and rebalancing inefficiencies, expansions, retracements, and consolidations."**
- **"I wake up every single morning knowing that I have one objective... If that one objective gets taken and I miss the trade, I'm done."** → confirms the no-trade rule.

### The three phases of price
- **"Three phases in price: distribute, rebalance, and manipulate."**
- **"Which ones do I like to trade? The rebalancing and the distribution."** Manipulation: **"I don't personally like to trade."**
- Advanced sequence: "we displace, rebalance, displace, reverse, and then maybe have a change in order [flow] for lower."
- Simplified for now: "two ways to go about it — displacements and rebalancing."

### Liquidity / draw mechanics
- Liquidity = "a swing high or a swing low at maybe a previous structure, or at a previous swing point low in an imbalance, or a pullback to continue your order flow."
- **Why liquidity builds at a swing low/high:** "Sell stops, orders." → **"the further you rebalance... more time, more orders, more stops."** (Time builds liquidity.)
- **Engineered high/low:** "Your rebalance or your distribution lower leaves liquidity at the high (or vice versa)." → this is how a target is created.
- Other valid objectives: **"equal highs, equal lows, range highs, range lows, previous day high/low, Asia high/low, London high/low."**
- Draw timeframes: order flow read on **"15 [minute] and above"**; HTF draws on "15 minute, one hour, two hour."

### Manipulation, SMT, fair-to-seek
- **Manipulation:** "when price manipulates an internal high or an external high before having your continuation lower or higher" — i.e. it happens in the *opposite* direction of bias, to stop people out.
- **SMT vs. fair-to-seek:** **"SMT is correlating to assets; fair-to-seek is an individual asset."** Both are *confluences, not entry reasons*: "a SMT or a fair-to-seek is a confluence. It's not a reason to take your trade."

### Entry / confirmation
- **"No buy sequence, no entry. No liquidity, no entry. You need the buy sequence"** (for a change in order flow). Must be a "clean buy sequence."
- "I don't just enter off the manipulation... you need a model."
- The "buy/sell sequence + closure" is the confirmation of a reversal/rebalance.

### Stops, targets, R
- **Protected stop logic:** three short-term lows below leave the next one as an **intermediate-term low → protected → keep your stop there.**
- Example trade: "shooting for about a 3R; you saw about a 2.5R." Trailing optional.
- **"PT1 should not get taken."** (If price pulls back to take PT1, the setup logic failed.)
- "Odds of retracing every time to your entry level? Quite low." → don't expect a clean fill to your exact level.
- **Trim:** "Do you ever trim? 95% no."

### Trade management
- **"Your trades should always be full size. Your losses are always full size."** → "or else your R will be non-existent."
- **"You don't partially take a loss."**
- **"When conditions are good, you trail. When they're bad, take 2R or 3R."**
- "It's not about how many trades you can win. It's about how many trades you can manage."

### FVG / rebalancing
- **FVG definition:** "an inefficiency off of a buy sequence that leaves orders in between"; "an imbalance delivery."
- **Use:** **"We use it for guidance. We don't actually use it for an entry."** "Your fair value gap only guides you... it just guides you into your draw." A failed move "is not because your FVG failed, that's because your bias failed."
- **Already-filled FVG:** "If it's already filled, I don't take it." (correct)
- **BPR:** "a sell sequence followed by an instant buy sequence, leaving a balanced price range" = "a stronger imbalance." (Bearish FVG immediately followed by bullish FVG, or vice versa.)

### Multi-timeframe cascade (the "A+ setup")
4H imbalance/manipulation → 2H same FVG (now "point of interest") → 30m POI → 5m fair-to-seek + reversal model → 1m model as backup. **"See how all this creates one A+ setup. The model doesn't change. Your bias doesn't change. Nothing changes."**

### Risk management & numbers (final topic)
- **Predict vs. react:** "Are we here to predict or react?" → React. Take "a slim of the pie multiple times without being wrong" rather than swinging for the full pie. **"You don't need the full pie."** Compounding > home-run trades.
- **"This only works with a hard risk approach."**
- His real numbers (TopStep / prop accounts): **risk ≈ $4,500 per stop-out; average winning trade $25,000–$50,000 per account.** "I don't even think twice about" a $4,500 loss.
- TPT-account rule example: 200k buffer → risk 20,000/trade, **"put a hard loss of 20,000. Don't do more, don't do less."** Safer: -10,000/account daily loss limit. **"I will never lose more than 10,000 in a day, but I can profit as much as I want."**
- **Trade WITH the daily loss limit, not around it.** He had an "8-day losing streak with all green trades" until he stopped fighting the drawdown rule.
- **"You can't bullshit your mathematics behind your trading."**
- **No Asia** — too-hefty pullbacks tick him out for full risk. **New York only.** Needs "good volatility, good volume, and clear objectives."
- **Win rate self-report:** "82% win rate in the discord" / "all-time, I think 82%." Trades **3–5 times a week**, doesn't post many.
- **Doesn't trade Mondays** — "they're low probability."

### Homework (assigned)
1. Each AM, mark your objective/liquidity for the session — and explain *why* (e.g. "Asia low, bearish order flow, multi-TF FVG, clean low").
2. Identify the phase (rebalancing or distribution), including HTF sequence (did we distribute in Asia → rebalance in NY?).
3. Screenshot before & after; apply a model if one formed; note how you managed it.
4. Minimum 3 trades by next Monday. **"Win rate is irrelevant... it's your risk or reward that matters the most."**
