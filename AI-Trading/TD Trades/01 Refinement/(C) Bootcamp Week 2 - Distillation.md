## What Week 2 is about (3 sentences)

Week 2 is a deep dive on **market structure** — Korabi's claim that price only ever does three things (**expand → pull back → continue**, i.e. distribute → rebalance → distribute) and that every trade is just figuring out which phase you're in. It introduces his **"protected high/low = 3 pullbacks"** definition (where the stop goes), his **"strong vs. weak highs"** range framework (mark only the two structural extremes, ignore the middle), and a hard-edged **trail-don't-trim** risk philosophy. The throughline: align multiple timeframes onto one Point of Interest, mark your high/low and objective, then have the patience to wait for the model and let the runner ride.

## Specific rules, numbers & definitions (verbatim where it matters)

### The 3 phases of price
- *"Price essentially does three things. We expand, we pull back, and we continue."* (0:01) — i.e. **distribution → rebalance → distribution**. Manipulation is a 4th, optional, lower-timeframe thing he mostly doesn't trade on the HTF.
- *"You're not gonna continue higher today until you have your rebalance lower."* (0:01) — **You must rebalance after distributing, before the next leg.** *"You have to rebalance before your second leg of distribution."* (0:04)

### Point of Interest (POI)
- *"A point of interest is an area where you changed your order flow or a market structure shift, and that area tends to be an imbalance, and it's where you wanna retrace into to form your model lower. So it's the best rebalance before distributing orders lower into your opposing pool liquidity."* (0:04)
- POI invalidation: *"It can only not become a point of interest if it doesn't form... usually if you inverse it, like you'll have a closure above. That's usually what I look for."* (0:06)

### Protected High/Low — the headline definition
- *"Protected low is three pullback lows... one pullback low, two pullback low, three pullback low... The third one gets created above this one and this one. So it's a short-term low, short-term low, short-term low. What confirms it? The closure and the rebalance. And then this one turns into an intermediate term low, which is protected."* (0:20–0:21)
- **Confirmation:** *"the third one gets created and holds below"* (0:23) — for a protected HIGH, the 3rd pullback high must form below the prior ones + a sell sequence + rebalance confirms it.
- **Use full pullbacks, not wicks:** *"Don't use wicks, because wicks obviously manipulate."* (0:23)
- **Stop placement:** stop goes at the protected high/low — NOT at the nearest internal wick. *"Put your stop where it's supposed to be and let your risk-reward do the work."* (0:23) *"This is also a protected low, which is where I'd preferably want to put my stop... 90% of the time you won't [take it]."* (0:17–0:19)

### Reversal model build (Asia → NY)
- Bias hack: *"watch Asia take your previous week low, previous daily low... any strong swing low previously."* (0:15)
- *"Your reversal model becomes from a previous structured high followed by a buy sequence and preferably if you can SMT or Failure to Seek at the low."* (0:16)
- Need a close below HTF FVG to displace lower; then look for reversal model at structure. (0:15–0:16)

### Manipulation vs Market Structure Shift / change in order flow
- *"Manipulation, you just wick and you continue, or you just chop in between it and you continue. A change in order flow or a market structure shift, you need a full buy sequence above it... You're not gonna see one candle closure and be like, all right, we reversed."* (0:18)
- **Manipulation definition (hard):** *"Manipulation is internally, for you to get ticked out of your position — it just stops out orders internally... Manipulation is you get manipulated and you still continue to the proper direction."* (0:57, 1:00)
- **Rebalance ≠ manipulation** (correction to student "Fox"): *"You need to go rebalance to fill orders, it's called a rebalance. Manipulation is you get manipulated and you still continue to the proper direction."* (1:00)

### Failure to Seek (FTS) — explained "like a five-year-old"
- *"Price failing to seek this low is bullish, because if you don't and you have a sell sequence below it, you have a change in order flow and back down you go. Why this low? Because this is your previous structured low before your full buy sequence higher."* (0:45)
- **You only have an FTS once you reverse:** *"You only fail to seek once you reverse, bro. And the first one you marked out is not a failure to seek."* (0:08)
- **Must be in a model/context:** *"You can't just mark any Failure to Seek high and then be like, oh, we're bearish now... a Failure to Seek comes off a large point of interest, you have a full reversal model, and then you Failure to Seek internally, which confirms for your bias to change lower."* (0:08)
- **FTS = individual asset (vs SMT = correlated pair):** *"It's an individual market asset... one asset fails to seek without looking at the other. SMT is correlating ES and NQ. Sometimes you'll just have an individual failure to seek."* (0:10)

### Strong vs Weak Highs (the range framework)
- *"Internally you have something called a range... Anything in the middle, do I care for? Most likely, no. I want to engineer a new high here."* (0:34)
- Mark only the two extremes: *"You have two important highs and lows... everything else is internal. Your job... will be to figure out which high or which low do we want."* (0:35, 0:40)
- To engineer a new high: *"I have to pull back and rebalance."* (0:34)
- Structured low definition: *"What creates a structured low? It's a previous pullback low and an imbalance before it [the] buy sequence higher."* (0:36)

### Risk management — TRAIL, don't trim
- *"Doesn't make sense, just trail. You lose full, you gain full... If you're trimming, you're ruining your risk, you're ruining your R."* (0:31)
- **Breakeven/PT structure:** *"My PT1's usually like 3R. But my first internal is breakeven, which is about 1R, 2R. And then you just trail it."* (0:32)
- **Trail mechanic:** move stop to the high above each new pullback low (in a short) / below each pullback high (in a long), every "good sell/buy sequence." (0:37–0:51)
- Trailing can stop you at 0.1–0.5R, *"who cares? But my wins are always full-size... 3R, 4R, 5R."* (0:33)
- *"Rarely I'll trim at last PT."* Trimming OK only above 2R–3R. (0:33, 0:37)
- **Hard stop loss: yes** (0:30). **No scaling in:** *"I don't like scaling in."* (1:05). **Market orders, not limits** (1:02).
- Live R examples: BE at 1.35R; 2.1R → 4.6R held; 3.6R regular vs **7.1R trailed** (up to 10R if tighter); when the last low breaks, pullbacks get aggressive → trail tighter / can trim. (0:37–0:52)

### Objective / bias
- *"Always have an objective. No objective, no trade. If you don't have an objective, makes no sense to take your trade — gambling, go to the casino."* (0:53)
- *"If you don't have a liquidity [target/monster], don't take it... if you have no liquidity target, don't even bother."* (0:59)
- **No indicators:** *"No indicators for your bias, rebalance, target."* (0:53)
- **TF alignment requirement:** *"No, just one — one large timeframe FVG out of structure, maybe."* (0:53)
- Execution: SMT/entry on the **1-minute**; HTF (1h+) is just POI / where the higher low sits. (0:10)

### When to stay away (4 conditions)
1. **Bad price** / objective taken right off the open. (1:00)
2. **The move already happened during Asia.** (1:00)
3. **News** (Trump tweets, red folder). (1:00)
4. **HTF accumulation** — *"I don't like to trade it."* (1:01)
- Also: size down until conditions pick up; *"a good objective only gives you one to two great opportunities."* (0:25, 0:56)

### Liquidity types to mark
- *"Swing highs, swing lows, CPI high/low, data highs/lows, session high/lows, London, Asia. That's it. Clean, effective."* (0:52)
- Data highs/lows get taken quicker (short-term, hold more orders in between). (0:54)
- External > internal in significance. (0:58)
