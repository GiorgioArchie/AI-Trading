Market structure refers to the way price moves and behaves in the market. It provides a framework to understand trends, reversals, and consolidations.

Downtrend: Lower highs (LH) and lower lows (LL).

  

Consolidation: Price moving sideways with no clear direction.

**The fair value gap**

  

- An FVG stands for Fair Value Gap, a concept commonly used in price action and institutional trading strategies. It refers to a price imbalance or gap in the market where price has moved quickly, leaving an area with little to no trading activity. This can be a sign of inefficiency in the market, where there may be unfilled orders or liquidity waiting to be tapped.

  

In the context of trading, an FVG typically appears when price moves through a specific area without any significant pullbacks or retracements. These gaps are often seen as potential areas for price to return to, as the market seeks to fill the imbalance (fill the gap) before continuing its trend.

  

Traders who use FVGs might look for price to revisit these areas to enter trades, expecting the market to balance out and fill the gap. It's an important concept in market structure analysis, particularly for those following institutional trading strategies or models.

**Order flow in price action**

  

  

  

"Failure to seek a low" and SMT (Smart Money Technique) Divergence are related concepts but have different meanings in trading.

  

This occurs when price fails to take out a previous low (or high), indicating a potential shift in market structure or liquidity.

  

Occurs best once a important low or high gets taken prior and price fails to seek new engineered liquidity below, indicating a possible shift in narrative.

  

Difference in Application

  

Failure to Seek a Low is an individual market behavior, it happens within a single chart when price refuses to take liquidity below a key low, or for continuation off a external.

  

SMT Divergence is a comparison between two correlated assets or instruments, highlighting strength/weakness imbalances.

  

  

Trade example.

  

A failure to seek a low or high confirms order flow in the direction you’re looking to trade. You should always align it with your draw and bias first — the failure to seek then acts as a confluence within a model, or as a sign that a model is developing. It can also lead into the formation of a protected low or high.

  

Price will often sweep a first external or internal low, and at times you might think it wants to return to that newly engineered low. In reality, price may fail to seek it and instead fully reverse higher. What you’re most likely missing is proper alignment with your order flow and staying committed to your initial bias. A failure to seek is confirmed by a clear shift in order flow back up or down on the lower timeframe.

Step By Step Approach

- identify the main trend of price action on HTF

  

  

- look for price to seek the ERLs before applying rev model

- look for a sweep of ERL and a creation of a structure low, before or after it gets taken

  

  

- after structure is identified, you will look for price and how it reacts at the high, you want quick moves off the low/high

  

  

- you will look for an impulsive reaction below that structure which will act as a neckline break

  

  

- after you shift in price i then apply the IRL ERL method for proper RR

  

  

- if price forms a clear fair value, BEFORE seeking your ERL you want price to retrace into it to provide you with fair value

  

  

- i personally like to take the flip candle and have a stop around 20p or previous wick high
